Warm-up

A thermometer for qubits

1 min

A thermometer doesn't list every molecule's speed; it gives one useful number. Quantum states have their thermometers too, called observables, and their averages, the expectation values, are the outputs of almost every quantum machine-learning model. Today: what they are, and how to measure them.

Big idea

Averages of ±1

4 min

Give each measurement outcome a value: +1 for 0 and −1 for 1. This measurement is called the observable ZZZ. Its average over many shots is its expectation value:

⟨Z⟩=(+1) P(0)+(−1) P(1)=P(0)−P(1)\langle Z\rangle = (+1)\,P(0) + (-1)\,P(1) = P(0) - P(1)⟨Z⟩=(+1)P(0)+(−1)P(1)=P(0)−P(1)

So ⟨Z⟩=+1\langle Z\rangle = +1⟨Z⟩=+1 for ∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩, −1-1−1 for ∣1⟩|1\rangle∣1⟩, and 000 for a fair coin like ∣+⟩|+\rangle∣+⟩. Here is the beautiful part: on the Bloch sphere, ⟨Z⟩\langle Z\rangle⟨Z⟩ is exactly the arrow's z-coordinate. Likewise ⟨X⟩\langle X\rangle⟨X⟩ and ⟨Y⟩\langle Y\rangle⟨Y⟩ are its x- and y-coordinates. The Bloch vector is (⟨X⟩,⟨Y⟩,⟨Z⟩)(\langle X\rangle, \langle Y\rangle, \langle Z\rangle)(⟨X⟩,⟨Y⟩,⟨Z⟩).

Hardware only measures 0/1, which is ZZZ. To measure XXX, rotate first: an H gate swaps the x- and z-axes, then a normal measurement reads what was the x-coordinate.

From NNN shots, the estimate of ⟨Z⟩\langle Z\rangle⟨Z⟩ wobbles by about (1−⟨Z⟩2)/N\sqrt{(1 - \langle Z\rangle^2)/N}(1−⟨Z⟩2)/N​: ±0.03 with 1,000 shots. On real devices every expectation value is an estimate.

With two qubits, products such as ZZZZZZ measure correlations: ZZ=+1ZZ = +1ZZ=+1 when the two bits agree and −1-1−1 when they differ. For the Bell state, ⟨Z⊗I⟩=0\langle Z \otimes I\rangle = 0⟨Z⊗I⟩=0 (each qubit alone is random) but ⟨ZZ⟩=+1\langle ZZ\rangle = +1⟨ZZ⟩=+1 (they always agree). In Qiskit these are written as Pauli strings, SparsePauliOp("ZZ"), and the Estimator primitive computes their expectation values.

Expectation values in Qiskit

cell 1 · three ways to get ⟨Z⟩
import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Statevector, SparsePauliOp

qc = QuantumCircuit(1)
qc.ry(1.0, 0)                       # tilt |0> by 1 radian
sv = Statevector(qc)
p = sv.probabilities()

print("P(0), P(1)            :", p.round(3))
print("<Z> = P(0) - P(1)     :", round(p[0] - p[1], 3))
print("<Z> computed by Qiskit:", round(float(np.real(sv.expectation_value(SparsePauliOp("Z")))), 3))
print("cos(theta)            :", round(np.cos(1.0), 3), "  <- the z-coordinate on the Bloch sphere")

cell 2 · the Estimator primitive
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from qiskit.circuit import Parameter
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorEstimator

theta = Parameter("θ")
ansatz = QuantumCircuit(1)
ansatz.ry(theta, 0)

angles = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 41).reshape(-1, 1)        # one row per parameter setting
estimator = StatevectorEstimator()
result = estimator.run([(ansatz, SparsePauliOp("Z"), angles),
                        (ansatz, SparsePauliOp("X"), angles)]).result()

plt.plot(angles, result[0].data.evs, "o-", ms=4, label=r"$\langle Z\rangle$")
plt.plot(angles, result[1].data.evs, "o-", ms=4, label=r"$\langle X\rangle$")
plt.xlabel("θ (radians)")
plt.ylabel("expectation value")
plt.title("RY(θ): the arrow's z and x coordinates")
plt.legend()
plt.show()

cell 3 · estimates from shots
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorSampler

measured = qc.copy()
measured.measure_all()
sampler = StatevectorSampler(seed=33)
for shots in [10, 100, 1000, 10000]:
    counts = sampler.run([measured], shots=shots).result()[0].data.meas.get_counts()
    estimate = (counts.get("0", 0) - counts.get("1", 0)) / shots
    print(f"{shots:>6} shots:  <Z> ≈ {estimate:+.3f}   (exact {np.cos(1.0):+.3f})")

cell 4 · measuring X
plus = QuantumCircuit(1)
plus.h(0)                                 # prepare |+>, which points along +x

in_z = plus.copy()
in_z.measure_all()
in_x = plus.copy()
in_x.h(0)                                 # rotate x onto z, then measure
in_x.measure_all()

print("|+> measured in the Z basis:", sampler.run([in_z], shots=1000).result()[0].data.meas.get_counts())
print("|+> measured in the X basis:", sampler.run([in_x], shots=1000).result()[0].data.meas.get_counts())

one solution
my_guess = {"|00>": 1 + 0.5 * 0, "Phi+": 1 + 0.5 * 1, "Psi-": -1 + 0.5 * (-1)}
for name, v in states.items():
    value = Statevector(v).expectation_value(H).real
    print(f"{name:>5}: predicted {my_guess[name]:+.2f}, Qiskit says {value:+.2f}")

Wrap-up

Today in one sentence

1 min
An expectation value is the average of an observable's ±1 outcomes; ⟨X⟩\langle X\rangle⟨X⟩, ⟨Y⟩\langle Y\rangle⟨Y⟩, ⟨Z⟩\langle Z\rangle⟨Z⟩ are the Bloch arrow's coordinates, ZZZZZZ measures correlation, and on hardware every one of them is estimated from shots.
Quantum link

A variational quantum classifier usually outputs f(x)=⟨Z⟩f(x) = \langle Z\ranglef(x)=⟨Z⟩ of the circuit's final state: a smooth number between −1 and +1 that depends on the input xxx and on the gate angles. Losses are built from it, and gradients come from evaluating it at shifted angles. Today's number is the quantum model's "logit".
