What do you remember?

What do θ\thetaθ and φ\varphiφ mean on the Bloch sphere?tap to flip
θ\thetaθ = latitude from the north pole: it sets P(0)=cos⁡2θ2P(0) = \cos^2\frac\theta2P(0)=cos22θ​. φ\varphiφ = longitude: the relative phase, invisible to a 0/1 measurement.
X, Y and Z as turns of the sphere?tap to flip
Half-turns (180°) about the x, y and z axes. X flips the bit, Z flips the phase, Y does both. Each one undoes itself.
Which states does Z leave alone?tap to flip
∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩|1\rangle∣1⟩, the two points on its axis (up to a global phase). Likewise X leaves ∣±⟩|\pm\rangle∣±⟩ alone and Y leaves ∣±i⟩|\pm i\rangle∣±i⟩ alone.
What does H do on the Bloch sphere?tap to flip
A half-turn about the diagonal between x and z: it swaps the z and x axes. ∣0⟩↔∣+⟩|0\rangle \leftrightarrow |+\rangle∣0⟩↔∣+⟩, ∣1⟩↔∣−⟩|1\rangle \leftrightarrow |-\rangle∣1⟩↔∣−⟩.
S, T, and why RX, RY, RZ matter?tap to flip
S and T are quarter and eighth turns about z (S2=ZS^2 = ZS2=Z). RX, RY, RZ turn by any angle: a continuous knob that can be trained.
How do you reach any one-qubit state from ∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩?tap to flip
RY(θ)R_Y(\theta)RY​(θ) for the latitude, then RZ(φ)R_Z(\varphi)RZ​(φ) for the longitude. Equal to the target up to a global phase.
