Unitary means reversible

A gate may move the state any way it likes, as long as the total probability stays 1. That one rule makes every gate a rotation, and every gate reversible.

Warm-up

Can you always go back?

1 min

Yesterday every gate became a matrix. But not every matrix can be a gate. Nature allows only one kind, and it comes with a surprising gift: every quantum gate can be undone, exactly. (A measurement can't, which is part of what makes it special.)

Big idea

The one rule

4 min

Whatever state goes in, the probabilities coming out must still add up to 1. So a gate must keep the length of every state vector. The matrices that do this are called unitary, and there's a neat test:

U†U=I,U†=U‾ T  (conjugate transpose: flip across the diagonal, conjugate every entry)U^\dagger U = I, \qquad U^\dagger = \overline{U}^{\,T} \ \ \text{(conjugate transpose: flip across the diagonal, conjugate every entry)}U†U=I,U†=UT  (conjugate transpose: flip across the diagonal, conjugate every entry)

In words: the columns of UUU have length 1 and are perpendicular to each other. Three consequences:

  • Every gate is reversible. The inverse is simply U†U^\daggerU†: U†U∣ψ⟩=∣ψ⟩U^\dagger U|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangleU†U∣ψ⟩=∣ψ⟩. For example H†=HH^\dagger = HH†=H, S†S^\daggerS† is a quarter turn the other way, and RY(θ)†=RY(−θ)R_Y(\theta)^\dagger = R_Y(-\theta)RY​(θ)†=RY​(−θ).
  • Undo a circuit backwards. Like socks and shoes: you put on socks, then shoes, but take off shoes first. The inverse of "A, then B, then C" is "C†C^\daggerC†, then B†B^\daggerB†, then A†A^\daggerA†".
  • Every one-qubit gate is a rotation of the Bloch sphere (up to a global phase). That's why last week's picture always worked.

What is not unitary? Anything that loses information. A matrix sending both ∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩|1\rangle∣1⟩ to ∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩ can't be undone, and indeed it breaks the total probability. A measurement is like that: after it collapses the state, there's no way back to the superposition. That's why measurements sit at the end of most circuits.

Quantum gates never forget: whatever they do, a unitary can undo. Only measurement throws information away.

cell 1 · the test U†U = I
import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Operator, Statevector

def dagger(U):
    """The conjugate transpose U†."""
    return U.conj().T

s = 1 / np.sqrt(2)
gates = {
    "H": np.array([[s, s], [s, -s]]),
    "S": np.array([[1, 0], [0, 1j]]),
    "T": np.array([[1, 0], [0, np.exp(1j * np.pi / 4)]]),
    "RY(40°)": np.array([[np.cos(np.radians(20)), -np.sin(np.radians(20))],     # RY(θ) uses θ/2 = 20°
                         [np.sin(np.radians(20)), np.cos(np.radians(20))]]),
}
for name, U in gates.items():
    print(f"{name:>8}: U†U = I ? {np.allclose(dagger(U) @ U, np.eye(2))}")

cell 4 · qc.inverse()
qc = QuantumCircuit(1)
qc.h(0)
qc.t(0)
qc.ry(0.7, 0)
undo = qc.inverse()
print("circuit:\n", qc.draw("text"))
print("its inverse:\n", undo.draw("text"))
both = qc.compose(undo)
print("circuit + inverse = identity?", Operator(both).equiv(Operator(np.eye(2))))
print("state at the end:", np.round(Statevector(both).data, 3) + 0)

Need a hint?

h stays h, s becomes sdg, t becomes tdg, and rx(θ) becomes rx(-θ).

Show a solution
one solution
inverse_name = {"h": "h", "s": "sdg", "t": "tdg", "x": "x"}
mine = QuantumCircuit(1)
for name, angle in reversed(steps):
    if angle is None:
        getattr(mine, inverse_name[name])(0)
    else:
        getattr(mine, name)(-angle, 0)          # rx(θ) is undone by rx(-θ)
print(mine.draw("text"))
print("mine undoes it?", Operator(qc.compose(mine)).equiv(Operator(np.eye(2))))
print("same as qc.inverse()?", Operator(mine).equiv(Operator(qc.inverse())))

Wrap-up

Today in one sentence

1 min
Gates are unitary matrices (U†U=IU^\dagger U = IU†U=I): they keep total probability at 1, they're always reversible with U†U^\daggerU†, and a circuit is undone in reverse order; only measurement destroys information.
Machine-learning link

Neural networks freely squash and throw away information (ReLU sets negatives to zero, for good). Quantum layers can't: until the final measurement, a quantum model is perfectly reversible. That's one reason quantum models are built so differently, with their non-linearity coming from data encoding and measurement.
