Three kinds of noise on the Bloch ball?tap to flip
Depolarizing shrinks the whole arrow; dephasing shrinks only its x and y parts; amplitude damping pulls everything towards ∣0⟩|0\rangle∣0⟩. On hardware they grow with time (T1T_1T1​, T2T_2T2​) and with every gate.
What do a density matrix's entries mean?tap to flip
The diagonal holds the probabilities of 0 and 1; the off-diagonal entries hold the phase. Trace 1, no negative eigenvalues, purity Tr ρ2\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^2Trρ2 from ½ to 1 for a qubit.
Two surprises about density matrices?tap to flip
Different recipes can give the same ρ\rhoρ (then no experiment tells them apart), and one half of a pure entangled pair is a mixed state.
Fidelity, trace distance and Helstrom?tap to flip
FFF: 1 for identical states. DDD: half the gap between the Bloch tips, 0 for identical. With one copy, the best guess between two equally likely states is right with probability 12(1+D)\frac12(1 + D)21​(1+D).
What does the transpiler do?tap to flip
Translates gates into the chip's native ones, places the qubits (layout), adds SWAPs for CNOTs between non-neighbors (3 CNOTs each), and optimizes. More CNOTs, more noise.
Readout mitigation and ZNE?tap to flip
Calibrate how often each state is misread and invert that matrix. Fold CNOTs to amplify gate noise (λ = 1, 3, 5), fit, and extrapolate to λ = 0. Both fix averages, at the price of extra shots.
