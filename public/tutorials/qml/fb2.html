What is a quantum kernel?tap to flip
k(x,x′)=∣⟨φ(x)∣φ(x′)⟩∣2k(x, x') = |\langle\varphi(x)|\varphi(x')\rangle|^2k(x,x′)=∣⟨φ(x)∣φ(x′)⟩∣2: encode both points, measure the fidelity of their states, and hand the numbers to an ordinary SVM. The circuit only measures similarity; the learning stays classical.
Why is it always a valid kernel?tap to flip
It equals Tr(ρ(x) ρ(x′))\mathrm{Tr}\big(\rho(x)\,\rho(x')\big)Tr(ρ(x)ρ(x′)), an inner product of density matrices. So the kernel matrix never has negative eigenvalues, exactly what an SVM needs.
How many circuits for a quantum-kernel SVM?tap to flip
N(N−1)2\frac{N(N-1)}{2}2N(N−1)​ for the training matrix (it's symmetric with ones on the diagonal) plus M×NM \times NM×N for test-versus-train, each estimated from shots.
Shot noise gave negative eigenvalues. Now what?tap to flip
Diagonalize, clip the negative eigenvalues to zero, and rebuild: the closest valid kernel matrix. Only the training matrix needs it.
The QSVM recipe?tap to flip
Shrink and scale on the training part, tune the bandwidth sss and CCC by cross-validation with precomputed kernels, open the test set once, and compare with an equally tuned RBF kernel.
Exponential concentration?tap to flip
With many qubits and an expressive map, different points get kernel values near 2−n2^{-n}2−n: too small for shots to tell apart, so the model can only memorize. A smaller bandwidth restores the spread, but may leave little that's quantum.
Three shortcuts that flatter a model?tap to flip
An untuned classical baseline, tuning on the test set, and reporting only the luckiest split. Friday's kernels went from 1 "win" to 10 out of 10 with all three, without one circuit changing.
The winner's curse?tap to flip
Pick the best-looking result out of many and part of its lead is luck. Retest it on fresh splits: ZZ's lead on breast cancer fell from about 1.1 to 0.5 points.
