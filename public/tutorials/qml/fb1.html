What do you remember?

CC, CQ, QC, QQ?tap to flip
Is the data classical or quantum, and is the algorithm classical or quantum? Quantum kernels and classifiers on ordinary data are CQ; ML on measurement records is QC; learning straight from quantum states is QQ.
The four steps of a quantum model?tap to flip
Encode the data into a state, process it with a (trainable) circuit, measure expectation values, and learn with a classical optimizer. And always compare with a strong classical baseline.
Angle encoding, and why scale?tap to flip
Each feature becomes RY(xj)R_Y(x_j)RY​(xj​) on its own qubit. Scale features to [0,π][0, \pi][0,π] first: angles repeat every 2π2\pi2π, and tiny ranges make every state look alike.
Amplitude encoding's promise and price?tap to flip
2n2^n2n numbers in nnn qubits. But preparing an arbitrary state takes about 2n2^n2n gates, reading amplitudes back takes many shots, and normalizing forgets the vector's length.
What does the ZZ feature map add?tap to flip
A phase 2(π−xi)(π−xj)2(\pi - x_i)(\pi - x_j)2(π−xi​)(π−xj​) that depends on two features at once: interactions and entanglement. Richer, wigglier similarities, not automatically better ones.
Swap test or inversion test?tap to flip
Swap test: any two states, P(0)=1+F2P(0) = \frac{1 + F}{2}P(0)=21+F​, one ancilla and controlled SWAPs. Inversion test: run U(x)U(x)U(x) then U(x′)†U(x')^\daggerU(x′)†, P(00…0)=FP(00\ldots0) = FP(00…0)=F; needs the circuits but no extra qubits.
